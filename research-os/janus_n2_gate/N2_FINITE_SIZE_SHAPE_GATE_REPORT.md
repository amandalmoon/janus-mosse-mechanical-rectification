# N2 Finite-Size / Compact-Shape Robustness Gate

## Decision
**PASS within the rigid compact-contact model.**

The tested result is not a new universal exponent. The supported statement is that compact self-similar contacts exhibit an inverse-linear-size similarity controlled by `Theta = theta sqrt(N)`, with small finite-size corrections. The result has been checked for the original ten compact hexagons and independently for larger hexagonal and disk-like families.

## 1. Canonical baseline
All calculations use the canonical unguided prepared-state contract: corrected 2H MoSSe three-shell GSFE, `k_perp=0`, and branch-followed depinning from the lowest-energy stable zero-force minimum.

## 2. Original ten-hex dense collapse
Sizes: `N = 37, 61, 91, 127, 169, 217, 271, 331, 397, 469`. Each forward and reverse fold was continued on a dense scaled-twist grid `Theta=0..30` in unit increments.

| scaled range | max rel. deviation +y | max rel. deviation -y | max rel. deviation rho |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Theta <= 18 | 0.2364% | 0.2305% | 5.844e-05 |
| Theta <= 24 | 0.4578% | 0.4508% | 7.051e-05 |
| Theta <= 30 | 0.7740% | 0.7734% | 7.051e-05 |

The worst deviations occur for the smallest contact, N=37, as expected for finite-size corrections.

## 3. Exact characteristic-angle scaling
For `q = 0.95, 0.90, 0.85, 0.80`, each characteristic angle was solved directly from the full fold equations at fixed `F = q F_c(0)`, rather than interpolated from the dense curves. The largest nonlinear residual was `8.096e-14` and the smallest hard Hessian mode was `11.636824`.

Across all eight direction/q fits,

- alpha range (all ten sizes): `0.503565411` to `0.503653301`
- minimum log-log R^2: `0.999992930`
- q=0.90: alpha_plus = `0.503626442`, alpha_minus = `0.503653301`.

### Removal of small contacts
For q=0.90, keeping only `N >= 217` gives

- alpha_plus = `0.501292289`
- alpha_minus = `0.501301680`.

Across all q and both directions with `N >= 217`, alpha lies between `0.501270629` and `0.501301680`. This monotonic approach toward 0.5 is the central reason to interpret the approximately 0.5036 full-range fit as a finite-size estimate of the expected 1/2 law, not as a distinct critical exponent.

An empirical `Theta_q = Theta_inf + c/N` sensitivity fit describes the ten-size correction extremely well (R^2 about 0.99996 for every q/direction), but no universal 1/N correction law is claimed without an analytic derivation.

## 4. Extended compact-shape test on a dense scaled grid
Hexagons: `N=127..1261` (8 sizes). Disks: `N=127..931` (6 sizes). Unlike the earlier four-point check, this audit evaluates every integer `Theta` from 0 through 20.

Maximum deviations over the whole dense interval:

- hex within-family: +y `0.0818%`, -y `0.0800%`
- disk within-family: +y `0.0230%`, -y `0.0222%`
- hex-vs-disk family means: +y `0.1048%`, -y `0.1022%`
- relative family difference in rho: `2.518e-05`.

All maxima occur at Theta=20, so there is no hidden intermediate-Theta loss of collapse.

## 5. Prepared-state validity beyond 3 degrees for the smallest contacts
The scaled interval Theta<=30 implies actual twists above 3 degrees for N=37, 61 and 91. These cannot be justified solely by the previous N=127 0-3 degree branch audit. We therefore independently enumerated stationary points and tested the dense-fold threshold immediately below and above the fold at N=37 (Theta=23,24,30), N=61 (24,30), and N=91 (24,30), in both force directions.

All `14/14` contracts passed: two zero-force stable minima are present, the lower-energy state remains separated from its competitor by at least `0.206436`, at least one stable minimum remains immediately below the reported prepared threshold, and no stable minimum remains immediately above it. The minimum soft-mode curvature at the below-fold probe is `0.096277`. Thus the wide scaled-range collapse is not being generated by an unverified last-surviving branch for the small-N endpoints.

## 6. Claim status
### VERIFIED within model scope
- forward and reverse threshold surfaces collapse under `Theta = theta sqrt(N)` for the tested compact hexagonal sequence;
- exact characteristic angles scale approximately as `N^-1/2`;
- the fitted exponent moves toward 1/2 when small contacts are removed;
- larger compact hexagonal and disk-like families agree to about 0.1% through Theta=20;
- the normalized directional split is even more size/shape invariant than the absolute thresholds;
- small-N high-Theta endpoints remain prepared-state depinning thresholds in the tested checks.

### NOT SUPPORTED / do not claim
- a new universal critical exponent 0.5036;
- shape independence for arbitrary compact geometries;
- applicability to rotated/non-self-similar boundaries (handled separately in N3);
- applicability to deformable or reconstructed contacts without the elasticity/atomistic robustness gates.

## Recommended manuscript sentence
> For compact self-similar rigid contacts, the forward and reverse prepared-state depinning surfaces are governed primarily by the scaled twist Theta = theta sqrt(N). Exact characteristic-angle fits over N=37-469 give alpha approximately 0.5036, while systematically excluding the smallest contacts drives alpha toward 1/2. Independent larger hexagonal and disk-like families agree at the approximately 0.1% level through Theta=20, supporting inverse-linear-size structure-factor similarity with finite-size corrections rather than a distinct universal exponent.
