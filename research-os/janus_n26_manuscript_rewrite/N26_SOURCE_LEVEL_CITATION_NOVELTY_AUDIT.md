## New adjacent prior art

### 27. Chen et al., J. Phys. Chem. Solids (2026)
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jpcs.2026.113940  
**Role:** Janus structural asymmetry as a tribological control variable.

The primary abstract reports high-throughput first-principles sliding-barrier calculations for MXY/MXY' and MXY/MX2 heterostructures. It shows that chemically distinct Janus faces can change interfacial sliding-barrier magnitudes, especially in MXY/MXY' systems.

**Novelty consequence:** It is no longer defensible to imply that using Janus structural asymmetry to tune sliding friction is itself novel. However, this paper compares interface configurations/barrier magnitudes; it does not report the present combination of finite prepared-ground-state opposite-direction depinning within one MoSSe GSFE, same-spectrum inversion controls, or zero-mean vector transport.

**Manuscript action:** Added to the Introduction as adjacent Janus tribology prior art, with the distinction stated explicitly.

### 28. Vijayan et al., arXiv:2609.02723 (2026)
**Title:** Twist as a Mechanical Switch for Reconfigurable Stacking in h-BN  
**Role:** adjacent registry-state switching under mechanical loading.

The preprint reports experimentally reproducible torque-driven switching between distinct commensurate h-BN stacking states and associated torque asymmetry.

**Novelty consequence:** Registry-state switching under mechanical control is not generically novel. The present result remains mechanically distinct because it is an equilibrium prepared-state selection under fixed imposed twist and translational loading; no torque-driven transition path or twist-sweep hysteresis is calculated.

**Manuscript action:** Added to Discussion 4.2 to sharpen the boundary of the triangular-boundary switching result.

### 29. Shao et al., arXiv:2609.08931 (2026)
**Title:** Edge-Dominated Twist Mechanics at van der Waals Interfaces  
**Role:** torsional edge mechanics and separation of twist from translational sliding.

The preprint reports edge-dominated torsional yielding and emphasizes that torsional and translational mechanical responses cannot be inferred from one another.

**Novelty consequence:** This strengthens, rather than weakens, the manuscript's existing scope boundary: fixed-twist center-of-mass depinning must not be extrapolated to applied-torque or rotational-relaxation protocols.

**Manuscript action:** Added to Discussion 4.2 as a direct reason not to generalize the present body-force thresholds to torque-controlled experiments.
