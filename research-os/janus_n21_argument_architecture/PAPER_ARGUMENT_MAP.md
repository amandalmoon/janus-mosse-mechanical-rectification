## Research question

How does the non-removable lateral registry inversion asymmetry of the published 2H Janus MoSSe GSFE translate into prepared-state directional depinning and zero-mean vector transport in an unguided finite two-dimensional contact, and which parts of that response survive geometry changes, thermal noise, and in-plane compliance?

## Central thesis

Within a DFT-anchored reduced finite-contact model, non-removable registry inversion asymmetry produces prepared-state direction-split unguided depinning; same-spectrum symmetry controls identify that asymmetry as the source of the directional response, while compact-contact scaling, boundary-state competition, vector locking, thermal statistics, and in-plane relaxation define the regimes in which the effect persists and the boundaries beyond which it has not been established.

## Mechanism

The model-level mechanism is not “Janus polarity implies a friction diode.” It is:

`published lateral GSFE -> non-removable inversion-odd registry content -> prepared full-2D directional stability landscape -> symmetry-controlled rectification -> protocol-dependent vector transport`.

Same-spectrum symmetrization and exact spatial inversion are the key discrimination tests. Geometry and preparation select which stable registry family is relevant; driving and noise then determine which dynamical observable is realized.

## Structural risks controlled

- phenomenon is shown **before** the same-spectrum causal/validation claim;
- compact-contact similarity is separated from boundary-driven registry switching;
- deterministic locking is separated from thermal mean-current persistence;
- exact winding probability is not conflated with directed transport;
- no physical polarization law is inferred from exact mathematical inversion;
- no “first friction diode/rocking ratchet” novelty claim;
- no universal material threshold, full atomistic validation, critical temperature, or calibrated device prediction.
