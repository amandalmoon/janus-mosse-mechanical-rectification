# Title

**Unguided Full-2D Mechanical Rectification and Vector Mode Locking in Finite Janus MoSSe Contacts**

# Abstract — replacement

Twist suppresses interfacial corrugation in many finite van der Waals contacts, but a lateral registry landscape lacking inversion symmetry can leave the residual pinning direction dependent. We analyze the published three-shell generalized stacking-fault energy (GSFE) of 2H Janus MoSSe in a finite contact whose center of mass is free in the full two-dimensional registry plane. A translation-minimized audit shows a non-removable inversion-odd registry component. For the lowest-energy zero-force preparation, the N=127 rigid contact at theta=1.5 deg depins at reduced forces F_c,+*=3.071135 and F_c,-*=1.258672. Matched same-spectrum symmetrization removes the global directional split, whereas exact spatial inversion swaps the thresholds and reverses the deterministic winding, supporting attribution of the directional response to registry inversion asymmetry within the reduced model. Tested compact hexagonal and disk-like contacts collapse closely under Theta=theta sqrt(N), while linear finite-element and nonlinear valence-force relaxations soften the absolute force scale without removing the directional sign. Rotated triangular boundaries instead produce competition between opposite-bias registry families, making the prepared response edge-state dependent. Under zero-mean rocking, the sampled map contains pinned and transporting integer winding states; representative transporting plateaus are Floquet attracting. Thermal noise rapidly mixes exact winding identity, yet trajectory-level inference resolves a weaker nonzero stationary mean current through the largest sampled T*=0.70. The results establish a prepared-state, protocol-conditioned mechanism within a DFT-anchored reduced model rather than a universal material threshold, physical polarity law, or calibrated device prediction.

# Keywords

janus transition-metal dichalcogenides; mechanical rectification; depinning; finite-contact scaling; structural lubricity; vector mode locking; rocking ratchet

# Introduction — replacement final contribution paragraph only

Against this background, we test a narrower question than whether a Janus label alone implies a friction diode: whether the actual published lateral 2H MoSSe registry landscape contains a non-removable inversion asymmetry that can organize prepared-state depinning and zero-mean motion in an unguided finite contact. We therefore separate the argument into successive falsifiable steps. First, we verify the GSFE provenance, preparation contract, and translation-minimized asymmetry. Second, we establish branch-resolved full-2D depinning before applying matched same-spectrum symmetrization and exact spatial inversion as mechanism-discrimination controls. Third, we test whether the response persists across compact size and shape variation and across two independent in-plane relaxation representations, while treating boundary-driven registry-state switching as a distinct geometry regime. Finally, we determine which deterministic vector-locked states occur under the declared rocking protocol and which observables survive thermal mode mixing. This ordering is intended to keep the contribution bounded: the study addresses a DFT-anchored reduced-model combination of registry asymmetry, finite-contact state selection, unguided rectification, and vector transport, without claiming the first friction diode or rocking ratchet, a universal material threshold, or a demonstrated physical polarity-reversal law.

# Results

## Published GSFE establishes a credible inversion-asymmetric registry input

The analysis starts from the published three-shell Fourier representation of the 2H Janus MoSSe GSFE and an unguided finite-contact contract in which the center of mass is free in both registry-plane coordinates. Unless stated otherwise, the contact is prepared in the lowest-energy stable zero-force minimum before loading. Figure 1a summarizes this preparation and loading geometry, whereas Figure 1b shows the conservative GSFE input used throughout the baseline calculation. The current source audit follows the published 2022 coefficients; the 2024 Erratum does not modify those GSFE coefficients or their conjugate phase pairing.

The material-level symmetry question is whether inversion asymmetry can be removed by a change of registry origin. Minimizing the inversion-odd content over translations gives A_min=0.0425361 and an odd RMS fraction of 0.206243 for the asymmetric 2H landscape. The reciprocal-triad diagnostic gives chi_1=0.583541. In contrast, the symmetric 3R controls reach numerical-zero odd content, while the asymmetric 3R control remains finite (Figure 1c). These diagnostics establish non-removable lateral registry inversion asymmetry for the published 2H surface. They do not establish that exact spatial inversion is equivalent to reversing a physical Janus polarization; that mapping remains outside the available first-principles input.

This schematic is illustrative and is not numerical evidence. ## Prepared ground-state branches exhibit unguided directional depinning

We next ask whether the declared prepared family actually loses stability at different forces under opposite loading. For the N=127 primitive contact, dense/adaptive continuation over theta=0-3 deg follows both the lowest-energy prepared family and the competing metastable family rather than selecting a threshold from the global envelope after the fact. The prepared family remains lower in zero-force energy across the sampled interval (Figure 2c), and its loss-of-stability force remains above that of the competing family in both directions (Figure 2d). Thus, the headline thresholds belong to one explicitly declared prepared state rather than to a twist-dependent switch between metastable basins.

The resulting directional split is large over the tested interval. For the prepared family, (F_c,+*,F_c,-*) changes from (3.485120,1.422236) at zero twist to (3.071135,1.258672) at theta=1.5 deg and (1.883315,0.782412) at theta=3 deg (Figure 2a,b). At theta=1.5 deg the static split is therefore Delta F_c*=1.812463. The stability audit supports generic fold language over most of the branch, but one isolated forward point near theta=2.97338566 deg and F*=1.91007353 has a higher-order degeneracy. We therefore use “depinning” or “stability boundary” globally and reserve “fold” for the generic branch segments where the local nondegeneracy conditions hold.

**Figure 2. Prepared-state unguided directional depinning.** (a,b) Positive- and negative-y branch-followed thresholds for the lowest-energy prepared family and a competing metastable family over 0-3 deg. (c) The positive zero-force energy margin U_meta-U_ground keeps the declared prepared family energetically selected. (d) Positive stability margins F_c^ground-F_c^meta in both directions show that the prepared family remains the last pinned survivor; the isolated higher-order stability degeneracy near theta=2.9734 deg is marked separately, so fold terminology is not extended to that point.

## Same-spectrum symmetry controls isolate registry asymmetry as the directional source

The directional phenomenon alone does not identify its conservative source. We therefore apply two same-spectrum mathematical interventions that alter the inversion-odd content without replacing the Fourier basis or introducing a different material model (Figure 3a). At theta=1.5 deg the original centered 2H landscape has global thresholds F_c,+*=3.071135 and F_c,-*=1.258672. Matched inversion-even symmetrization gives F_c,+*=1.7676995593 and F_c,-*=1.7676995587, leaving a residual split of only 6.57 x 10^-10. Exact spatial inversion swaps the original thresholds to F_c,+*=1.258672 and F_c,-*=3.071135 (Figure 3b).

The deterministic rocking response follows the same contract. The original landscape supports the canonical winding (m,n)=(1,-1), the matched symmetrized landscape is pinned at (0,0), and exact inversion gives (-1,1) (Figure 3c). Threshold-swap and winding-reversal residuals remain at numerical precision (Figure 3d). These interventions therefore support attribution of the directional response to registry inversion asymmetry within the declared reduced model. They are mechanism-discrimination controls, not a claim that an experimentally reversed Janus polarization realizes the exact inverted mathematical surface.

**Figure 3. Same-spectrum symmetry controls isolate the model-level mechanism.** (a) Original, matched inversion-even, and exact-inverted registry surfaces/operations at fixed spectral content. (b) Global depinning thresholds F_c,+ and F_c,-: matched symmetrization makes them degenerate, whereas exact inversion swaps them. (c) The deterministic winding changes from (1,-1) to (0,0) under matched symmetrization and to (-1,1) under exact inversion. (d) Numerical residuals for the matched threshold split, matched winding norm, threshold-swap error, and winding-reversal error. These are mathematical mechanism-discrimination controls within the reduced model, not a demonstrated physical polarity-switching law.

## Compact scaling and in-plane compliance preserve the directional sign

Having isolated the model-level source, we test whether the response is an artifact of one rigid compact contact. For compact hexagonal contacts, the characteristic twist scale approaches the expected theta sqrt(N) similarity as size grows. An independently constructed disk-like compact family exhibits the same normalized trend. Across the dense Theta=theta sqrt(N)=0-20 grid, the largest within-family deviations and the difference between hexagonal and disk-family means remain at approximately 0.1% or below (Figure 4a,b). The collapse supports a compact-contact structure-factor description over the tested families; it does not imply shape independence for arbitrary boundaries.

Internal compliance changes the absolute force scale but not the directional sign in the tested models. At theta=1.5 deg the nominal linear finite-element membrane gives F_c,+*=2.934302 and F_c,-*=1.190210, with rho=0.422860. The independently reconstructed nonlinear bond-angle valence-force model gives 2.935400 and 1.190479, with rho=0.422921. FEM stiffness variation from C/2 to 2C and an alternative elastic-constant pair moves the absolute thresholds, while the 100C calculation approaches the rigid limit; the normalized split remains close to the rigid value across the tested representations (Figure 4c,d). The two relaxation models therefore rule out the strongest version of a rigid-contact artifact, but they include in-plane compliance only and do not constitute full three-dimensional atomistic validation.

(b) Maximum within-family size deviations and hex-versus-disk mean differences remain below approximately 0.1% through Theta=20. (c) At theta=1.5 deg, nominal linear FEM and nonlinear bond-angle VFF soften the absolute thresholds relative to the rigid model while retaining F_c,+>F_c,-; blue circles and orange squares denote F_c,+ and F_c,-. (d) The normalized split rho remains close to the rigid baseline across the tested FEM stiffness variants and VFF representations. These tests cover in-plane compliance and selected compact shapes, not arbitrary boundaries or full 3D atomistic relaxation.

## Boundary-driven registry competition can reverse the prepared response

Compact-family similarity does not extend automatically to boundaries that change which registry family is selected. For fixed-N=127 triangular contacts with edge orientations of 20 and 25 deg, two stable low-energy registry families coexist. Their zero-force energy difference changes sign at theta=2.92127801 deg and 2.84723312 deg, respectively (Figure 5a), while the two families carry opposite directional threshold splittings (Figure 5b). Re-preparing the lower-energy family at each fixed twist therefore reverses the prepared-state sign when the equilibrium registry changes (Figure 5c).

The location of this switch is not universal. Varying the relative local-GSFE weight assigned to the 48 outer sites from 0.5 to 1.5 moves the equilibrium crossing substantially and can move it outside the original 0-3 deg interval (Figure 5d). The result therefore supports a boundary-mediated state-selection mechanism, not a universal switching angle. Because no transition barrier, twist-sweep trajectory, or hysteresis loop was computed, the sign change is an equilibrium re-preparation result rather than a demonstrated kinetic switching event.

**Figure 5. Boundary-induced registry-state switching.** (a) Zero-force energy differences U_B-U_A locate equilibrium crossings for 20 and 25 deg triangular edge orientations. (b) Registry families A and B carry opposite branch-followed directional splittings. (c) Selecting the lower-energy family at fixed twist reverses the prepared-state sign at the crossing; no dynamical trajectory across the discontinuity is implied. (d) Changing the outer-site energy weight shifts the crossing and can move it outside the original 0-3 deg window. The exact switch angle is therefore edge-model sensitive.

## Zero-mean rocking produces vector-locked relative-periodic states

We then ask whether the validated asymmetric landscape can organize motion under zero-mean longitudinal forcing. At theta=1.5 deg with reduced mass m*=1 and damping gamma*=4, a 13 x 7 grid spans F_0*=1.0-4.0 and tau*=20-80. Cycle-resolved classification shows integer winding pairs at all 91 sampled conditions, but these states include both pinned (0,0) and transporting windings (Figure 6a,b). Along tau*=40, the sampled response passes through a staircase that includes (0,0), (1,-1), (1,-2), (2,-3), and higher-winding states as the rocking amplitude increases (Figure 6c). The map is therefore evidence for sampled protocol-specific vector locking, not for transport at every sampled point or for a continuous phase diagram.

The canonical (1,-1) plateau satisfies the stronger relative-periodic-orbit tests. Independent DOP853 and Radau monodromy calculations give a leading Floquet spectral radius rho_F approximately 6.31 x 10^-22 and one-cycle closure of order 10^-11 (Figure 6d). Additional representative plateaus also satisfy rho_F<1 under the declared checks. These results establish local attraction for tested relative-periodic states; they do not establish global uniqueness, and a separate damping sweep shows that the winding map reorganizes substantially as gamma* changes.

**Figure 6. Vector-locked relative-periodic response under zero-mean rocking.** (a,b) Integer windings m and n across the 91 sampled amplitude-period points at theta=1.5 deg; the map contains pinned (0,0) and transporting states. (c) The tau*=40 slice shows the sampled vector staircase. (d) Independent DOP853 and Radau monodromy calculations give a leading Floquet spectral radius near 6.31 x 10^-22 and relative-periodic closure of order 10^-11 for the representative (1,-1) orbit. The map is a sampled protocol-specific state map, not a continuous phase diagram or a proof of global uniqueness.

## Thermal noise destroys exact winding before erasing the stationary mean current

Finite-temperature behavior is evaluated from stationary long-window trajectories, with the trajectory rather than the individual cycle used as the inference unit. The full T*=0.02-0.70 series uses N=500 trajectories after 60 burn cycles and 100 measured cycles; the high-temperature band T*=0.50-0.70 is independently replicated with a second N=500 seed ensemble and pooled to N=1000. Mean u and v decrease in magnitude with temperature but remain directed over the sampled range (Figure 7a). At T*=0.70 the pooled estimate is (<u>,<v>)=(0.036998,-0.076448), and the trajectory-level bootstrap gives 95% intervals [0.01265,0.06132] and [-0.10088,-0.05195], respectively.

The stronger joint-vector test reaches the same bounded conclusion. For each pooled high-temperature point, the Hotelling statistic exceeds the declared 95% critical value; at T*=0.70 the ratio remains about 6.3 (Figure 7b). Exact deterministic mode identity is much more fragile: the probability of the canonical (1,-1) cycle falls to approximately 0.011 at T*=0.70, while P(v_cycle<0)=0.50802 with bootstrap 95% interval [0.50506,0.51100] (Figure 7c). Thus, exact winding identity is largely mixed before the weaker stationary directed mean vector becomes statistically unresolved. Targeted timestep checks support the high-temperature effect size over the tested integration steps, but no thermal critical temperature, equilibrium transition, or extrapolation beyond T*=0.70 is inferred.

(b) For the pooled high-temperature samples, Hotelling T^2 divided by its declared 95% critical value remains above one through T*=0.70, so the sampled two-component zero-current vector is rejected throughout this band. # Discussion

## Registry asymmetry provides a model-level source of prepared-state directionality

The results support a specific mechanism within the reduced finite-contact model. The published lateral GSFE contains inversion-odd registry content that cannot be removed by translation; a lowest-energy prepared finite contact built from that surface has direction-split full-2D stability boundaries; and matched same-spectrum interventions remove or reverse the static and dynamic directional observables as required by the proposed symmetry mechanism. This chain is stronger than inferring directionality from the Janus label or from a comparison between chemically different stackings because the intervention changes the relevant symmetry content while holding the model class fixed.

The scope of that mechanism remains state and protocol conditioned. The competing metastable basin can carry the opposite signed static split, and the force-direction scan is anisotropic. Likewise, deterministic winding depends on the declared inertial and damping parameters. “Unguided” therefore means that no added transverse confinement is required for the baseline response; it does not mean preparation independent, loading-axis independent, damping independent, or universal across mechanical protocols. Exact spatial inversion is also only a mathematical control because the available calculations do not provide a physical polarization-to-GSFE map.

## Compact similarity and boundary state selection define two geometry regimes

The geometry tests separate two behaviors that should not be conflated. Within tested compact self-similar families, theta sqrt(N) organizes the normalized depinning curves, and an independent disk-like family remains close to the hexagonal result. The in-plane FEM and nonlinear VFF calculations then show that internal compliance can renormalize the absolute force scale without removing the directional sign. Together these results support a compact-contact mechanism that is not tied to one rigid N=127 realization, while also ruling out interpretation of the rigid thresholds as universal material constants.

Rotated triangular boundaries probe a different question because they alter the relative energy of competing registry families. Once opposite-bias minima become near-degenerate, the equilibrium prepared state can switch and the sign can reverse even at fixed material parameters. The substantial motion of the crossing under outer-site reweighting is therefore not a nuisance to be averaged away; it is evidence that noncompact edges can control state selection in ways that compact similarity does not capture. The exact reversal angle remains edge-model dependent until a higher-fidelity treatment of edge reconstruction, chemistry, and nonlocal relaxation is available.

## Static rectification and dynamic transport share a symmetry source but remain nonidentical

The static threshold inequality is a prerequisite for the present rocking response, not a complete dynamical phase diagram. Under the declared m*=1, gamma*=4 protocol, the same asymmetric prepared landscape supports a mixture of pinned and transporting integer winding states, and representative transporting states are strongly attracting relative-periodic orbits. Changing damping reorganizes those windings, so the vector map cannot be interpreted as a material-only property of MoSSe.

Thermal trajectories sharpen this distinction further. Exact cycle identity is lost quickly, but the long-window trajectory mean can remain statistically directed. The approach of P(v_cycle<0) toward one-half while the joint mean vector remains nonzero shows that weak transport need not be represented by frequent returns to the deterministic target winding. The appropriate thermal statement is therefore a hierarchy of observables over a sampled protocol, not a universal critical temperature or a claim that deterministic mode locking survives intact to T*=0.70.

## Model and physical-mapping boundaries

Several unresolved steps separate the present model result from an experimental constitutive law. The sitewise local-registry construction omits collective out-of-plane corrugation, buckling, atomistic edge reconstruction and chemistry, defects, and nonlocal edge energetics. The two internal-relaxation extensions include in-plane compliance only. A public empirical interlayer candidate was retained as a qualitative stress test rather than promoted to quantitative validation because it does not satisfy the published higher-harmonic GSFE contract; a higher-level Janus-specific interatomic model would need to reproduce the same lateral energy surface before it could serve as an independent quantitative check.

The calculations also impose fixed twist and spatially uniform center-of-mass/body-force loading. Rotational relaxation, torque, spring pulling, localized edge or center loading, participating mass, dissipative calibration, and experimental state preparation are not determined by the present GSFE. Reduced time and temperature therefore cannot be converted to laboratory frequency or kelvin without additional calibration. Finally, no physical polarization-reversal calculation is available to connect a change in Janus polarity to the exact spatial inversion used here. These are boundaries on interpretation rather than hidden assumptions supporting the headline claim.

# Conclusions — replacement

A finite contact built from the published 2H Janus MoSSe GSFE exhibits prepared-state direction-split depinning in the full two-dimensional registry plane without an added transverse guide. For the lowest-energy N=127 rigid preparation at theta=1.5 deg, the reduced thresholds are F_c,+*=3.071135 and F_c,-*=1.258672. Translation-minimized asymmetry diagnostics together with matched same-spectrum symmetrization and exact-inversion controls support attribution of this response to non-removable lateral registry inversion asymmetry within the reduced model. The validated scope is broader than one rigid contact but narrower than a universal material law: tested compact shapes follow a common scaled-twist trend, linear FEM and nonlinear VFF relaxations preserve the directional sign while softening absolute thresholds, and noncompact triangular boundaries can instead switch the selected registry family and reverse the prepared response. Under the declared zero-mean rocking protocol, the sampled state map contains both pinned and transporting integer windings, with representative transporting plateaus satisfying relative-periodic and Floquet attraction checks. Thermal noise strongly mixes exact winding identity, yet a weaker stationary mean vector remains statistically resolved through the largest sampled T*=0.70. The present result is therefore a state-, geometry-, and protocol-conditioned mechanism for unguided rectification and vector transport in a DFT-anchored finite-contact model; it does not establish a universal threshold, a physical polarity-reversal law, full 3D atomistic validation, or an absolute experimental time/temperature scale.
